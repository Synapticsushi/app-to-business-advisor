# Commercialization framework

## Pricing decision memo

Use this structure when a founder asks for a price or packaging recommendation:

1. **Decision:** recommended offer, buyer, billing shape, and initial range.
2. **Evidence:** verified comparable products, buyer workflow, alternative cost, and cost-to-serve floor.
3. **Assumptions:** the facts that would materially change the answer.
4. **Validation:** specific conversations or experiments, expected signal, and the decision date.
5. **Risks:** procurement, implementation, support load, privacy/security, and concentration risk.

Avoid unsupported market-wide percentages. Re-check competitor pricing close to the customer-facing use date and link to the primary pricing page or quote.

## Design-partner checklist

For a production pilot, clarify:

- The tested workflow and excluded work.
- The price or consideration, discount, normal rate, and duration.
- Named users and the buyer/decision-maker.
- Measurable adoption and success criteria.
- Feedback cadence, data access, and support boundaries.
- Start date, end date, conversion meeting, and outcomes if it does not convert.
- Permission and scope for testimonial, reference, or case-study use, if any.
- Ownership and licensing of custom work, integrations, and data.

Use a free evaluation only when the scope, duration, learning goal, and non-production boundary are explicit. Do not use it as a substitute for an open-ended implementation engagement.

## Contract and support review prompts

Ask a qualified lawyer to review the agreement, especially when the founder built the product while working for the buyer or a related party. That review should address employment/contractor obligations, work-for-hire risk, confidentiality, and IP ownership.

Before proposing an SLA, record the product's actual service boundaries: which functions work offline, what depends on cloud sync, event-day coverage, monitoring, maintenance constraints, and recovery procedure. Express commitments as measurable operations rather than borrowed industry percentages.

For customer data, identify data categories, storage location, vendor access, retention, export format, deletion process, subprocessors, incident response, and who owns the authoritative record after termination.
